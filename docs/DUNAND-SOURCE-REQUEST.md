Subject: Access and rights-routing request for Dunand's Byblos inscriptions

Dear Library / Antiquities Publications team,

I’m building a free, noncommercial research corpus of ancient scripts and need to collate Maurice Dunand’s editions of the Byblos inscriptions. Could you help me obtain lawful copies of these two publications, or direct me to a library or rights holder that can?

1. *Byblia Grammata: documents et recherches sur le développement de l’écriture en Phénicie*, Beyrouth, 1945 (also catalogued as 1946), OCLC 1191221, xix + 200 printed pages. The core inscription discussion is cited at pp. 71–138; I need its referenced figures, sign-number tables, captions, plates, and additions/corrections as well as the text.
2. “Nouvelles inscriptions pseudo-hiéroglyphiques découvertes à Byblos,” *Bulletin du Musée de Beyrouth* 30 (nominal year 1978, appeared 1981), pp. 51–59, four figures and two plates. Some bibliographies give pp. 52–58, so the broader page range and all figures/plates would help avoid missing material.

Library lending, a permitted document-supply copy or a purchased copy would all be useful. We will keep printed page/plate labels distinct from scan-page numbers and preserve the edition’s uncertainties rather than treating our transcription as independently verified.

Separately, could you identify who can authorize private OCR and AI-assisted collation (including whether hosted AI processing is permitted or must stay local), publication of derived catalogue/numbering concordances and comparison annotations, and reproduction of systematic readings, sign tables, drawings or images? These uses can be considered separately. We can keep any restricted components private and follow the supplied attribution, credit lines and reuse terms. Please let us know any copy fees or permissions requirements before supply.

Thank you for any access route or appropriate contact you can provide.
