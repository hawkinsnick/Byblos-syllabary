## Draft request to OCBI rights holders

We are preparing a public, attributed research corpus of the undeciphered Byblos
script at https://github.com/hawkinsnick/Byblos-syllabary. We have consulted your
provider metadata but have not redistributed your sign sequences, fonts or plates.
Could you specify the applicable licence or grant written permission for the
following uses, separately where the rights differ?

- Redistribute machine-readable sequences in a public repository and downloadable
  research exports, preserving your edition identifiers and alternative readings.
- Distribute the exact encoding map needed to interpret those sequences, including
  any Private Use Area mapping; clarify font rights separately.
- Publish corrections and normalization logs as distinct, attributed readings,
  retaining the original imported sequence and its version.
- Identify who controls the slide photographs and published drawings, and whether
  they may be included or must remain external references.

Please provide the exact licence, required attribution, permitted modifications,
redistribution conditions, version covered and any excluded material. We would also
welcome the item-level crosswalk behind the reported corpus counts, references for
the tentative fragments, and clarification of the b'c plate link and inscription m's
reading direction. Your permission would not be presented as scientific endorsement.

## Independent specialist brief

Review the frozen repository commit and the evidence digest from `byblos audit`.
Declare relevant expertise, affiliations, contributions to this corpus and conflicts.
Reviewers must be independent of the project work being approved. They may assess
their own published arguments critically, but source authorship alone is not approval
of this corpus. The final reviewer should assess the adequacy of the whole search
and coverage process, including exclusions and unresolved alternatives.

Deliver a dated report for each scoped record/sequence and a final admission report:

1. Check object identity, excavation versus museum identifiers, surfaces and joins.
2. Compare each line and token against identified evidence, including damage,
   gaps, uncertain alternatives, restoration, separators and direction.
3. Check edition-specific sign labels and variant handling; reject unsupported
   phonetic assignments as canonical metadata.
4. Evaluate every membership decision and source exclusion, conflicting count,
   bibliography disposition, candidate duplicate and proposed fragment.
5. Assess attribution, provenance, encoding and declared reuse terms; identify
   any rights question needing clarification from the relevant rights holder.
6. State approval or requested corrections and its limits. Cite precise evidence.

The report must identify the reviewed commit and `review_evidence_sha256`.
Store the durable report/reference before recording approval. Recompute the digest
after any evidence correction and obtain renewed approval. Passing tests, author
correspondence, a repository star or general encouragement is not specialist review.

## Work after acquisition

Register the acquired editions and their actual consultation depth. Establish
physical identities and complete face inventories before tokenization. Produce
one full, uncertainty-preserving draft per edition; record source versions, creators,
rights and every normalization. Independently collate every line. Keep alternatives
and disputed additions separate. Finish source/count/bibliography dispositions and
the systematic search audit, then submit the frozen bundle for final admission review.
